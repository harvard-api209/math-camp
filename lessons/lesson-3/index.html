THE CHANGING LABOR MARKET

Policy analysts need five complementary capabilities.

The lesson begins by separating the components of an agent workflow, then asks which capabilities MPA/ID graduates need as coding agents become more capable. Students rank policy framing, statistical judgment, data stewardship, coding fluency, and agent supervision.

Employers expect a substantial share of workers’ core skills to change by 2030, while analytical thinking remains a leading core skill. OECD research finds broad demand in AI-exposed jobs for management, cognitive, social, and digital capabilities; specialized machine-learning skills apply to a smaller set of roles.

In Pocock’s vocabulary, the harness executes tool calls and returns results to the model. The model proposes; the environment records what happened; the analyst reviews the evidence. The course’s claim-audit skill supplies a procedure and verification standard without changing the model’s parameters. See Matt Pocock’s AI Coding Dictionary.

Those skills are applied to one briefing question: across 2000–2022, how did national income and under-five mortality move together across economies and within economies? Which associations does this evidence support in a briefing to a policy director?

The analysis uses 4,296 economy-years, 188 economies, and 23 years. These observations support comparisons across economies in the same year and comparisons within economies over time. The exercise estimates descriptive associations. Identification of an income intervention requires a separate research design.

FIGURE, THEN MODELS

The specification determines the variation used to estimate the coefficient.

Students use Slido to predict the pattern, then build the figure in RStudio. The first figure pools all economy–years in one neutral point cloud, with GDP per capita on the horizontal axis and under-five mortality on the vertical axis. Selected trajectories then show changes over time.

The first model is a pooled log–log summary. A 10% income difference is associated with roughly 7.8% lower under-five mortality across all economy-years. The class then compares pooled OLS with a year fixed-effects specification and an economy and year fixed-effects specification. The latter coefficient is identified by within-economy changes after common year movements are absorbed.

INTERPRETATION

The year fixed-effects estimate is nearly unchanged from pooled OLS. The coefficient becomes smaller after economy fixed effects are added. The three specifications describe pooled, within-year, and within-economy associations; they do not identify the causal effect of income growth on mortality.

Equatorial Guinea provides the country case. In 2008, reported GDP per capita was approximately $39,995 in constant PPP terms and under-five mortality was 118 deaths per 1,000 live births; the pooled model predicted approximately 7.4. Students inspect the indicator definitions and then identify the distributional, institutional, service-delivery, and timing evidence required to explain the discrepancy.

Removing Equatorial Guinea–2008 changes the pooled 10% comparison from approximately −7.755% to −7.760%. The calculation shows that this observation does not determine the pooled coefficient. It does not explain the country’s trajectory.

CODING AGENTS

Check one briefing sentence against the saved results.

The Codex request names the sentence, the files the agent may read, the checks it must perform, and the evidence required for each conclusion.

The project includes .agents/skills/policy-claim-audit/. During the lesson, the class checks a sentence that combines a supported fixed-effects estimate with an unsupported causal conclusion. In Lab 3, students first compare the all-countries evidence with one selected region and save their own interpretation. Codex then reads that completed file, fits two bounded pooled models, and proposes claims that students verify against their tables, figures, and calculations.

CLASSROOM SEQUENCE

Slides state the statistical question. Slido records a prediction. RStudio produces the relevant evidence. Codex reviews the saved files. The class returns to the deck to accept, revise, or reject one agent statement.
