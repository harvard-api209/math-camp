\section{From a question to a table}
\subsection{Define the object}

\begin{frame}{Begin with the policy question}
  Imagine that a development-policy team is preparing a short briefing on
  economic resources and child survival. It wants to know where higher income
  coincides with lower mortality---and which economies do not follow the broad
  pattern. To represent every economy--year once, the briefing table should
  contain one row for each economy and year.

  \vspace{0.8em}
  \begin{center}
    {\Large\color{crimson}How is national income associated with under-five
    mortality across economies from 2000 to 2022?}
  \end{center}

  \begin{block}{Two minutes with someone near you}
    Suppose Kenya--2022 appears in two rows of the draft analysis table. What
    could have produced two rows for the same economy and period? Why could
    that change the briefing's table, plot, or conclusion?
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A question implies a data object}
  Our question relates two measurements observed for the same economy and year:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item GDP per capita, PPP;
    \item under-five mortality per 1,000 live births.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.5em}
  To compare them directly, both values must be attached to the same
  \alert{economy--year observation}.

  \vfill
  One row is not a formatting preference. It is a claim about what counts as one
  observation in the analysis.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The source can legitimately have two Kenya--2022 rows}
  \small
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.32}
  \begin{tabular*}{\textwidth}{@{\extracolsep{\fill}}llll@{}}
    \toprule
    Country & Year & Indicator & Value \\
    \midrule
    Kenya & 2022 & GDP per capita, PPP & 5,492 \\
    Kenya & 2022 & Under-five mortality & 40.5 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular*}

  \vspace{0.8em}
  Here one row means an \alert{economy--year--indicator}. The complete key is:
  \begin{center}
    \texttt{iso3c + year + indicator}
  \end{center}

  Two Kenya--2022 rows are expected because the indicator distinguishes them.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The analysis table needs one Kenya--2022 row}
  \small
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.32}
  \begin{tabular*}{\textwidth}{@{\extracolsep{\fill}}llll@{}}
    \toprule
    Country & Year & GDP per capita, PPP & Under-five mortality \\
    \midrule
    Kenya & 2022 & 5,492 & 40.5 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular*}

  \vspace{0.8em}
  Here one row means an \alert{economy--year}. The complete key is:
  \begin{center}
    \texttt{iso3c + year}
  \end{center}

  The same evidence is now arranged for the comparison in our policy question.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A duplicate is defined relative to a key}
  \begin{block}{Long source table}
    Two country--year rows can be correct when \texttt{indicator} is part of the
    unit and key.
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Wide analysis table}
    Two country--year rows violate the table plan when \texttt{iso3c + year}
    is supposed to identify each observation once.
  \end{block}

  \vfill
  The useful question is not simply ``Are there duplicate rows?'' It is ``Does
  the proposed key uniquely identify the unit we intend to analyze?''
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Return to the opening question}
  What could produce two Kenya--2022 rows, and why could the briefing change?

  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item<1-> \textbf{Long source.} One row per indicator is legitimate when
      \texttt{indicator} belongs to the key; reshape before comparing the measures.
    \item<2-> \textbf{Unnamed dimension.} Sex, unit, or version may distinguish
      observations that the draft table plan has incorrectly combined.
    \item<3-> \textbf{Nonunique join key.} A repeated Kenya record on the right
      can multiply every Kenya year and give those observations excess weight.
    \item<4-> \textbf{Repeated record.} Importing or appending the same evidence
      twice can duplicate a point and alter a summary, plot, or model.
  \end{enumerate}

  \vfill
  \uncover<5->{Two rows are not inherently wrong. They are wrong when they
  violate the unit and key required by the policy question. Diagnose the
  difference before using \texttt{distinct()}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Write down the table we need}
  We will call this short list our \alert{table plan}:

  \vspace{0.25em}
  \begin{description}
    \item[Population] Economy--years with both measures observed and positive income.
    \item[Unit] One economy--year.
    \item[Key] \texttt{iso3c + year}.
    \item[Period] 2000--2022.
    \item[Outcome] Under-five mortality per 1,000 live births.
    \item[Comparison] GDP per capita, PPP.
  \end{description}
\end{frame}

  \vfill
  Every consequential line of R should connect to one decision in the table
  plan or to evidence that the constructed table follows it.
\end{frame}
